**Tagline:** *"Know what your AI costs."* (On edos.io: *"Know what your AI costs, and who's spending it."*)

## What it is, in one sentence

> **Ork Meter Enterprise gives a whole company one view of its AI agent usage and estimated
> cost, by department, team and project, so finance, IT and engineering all work from the same numbers.**

## The three promises: See, Own, Prove

1. **See: every token, attributed.** Usage and estimated cost broken down by department, team,
   project group and cost center. It keeps three things clearly separate: what was *observed*,
   what is *estimated*, and what the AI provider actually *charged*.
2. **Own: budgets every team can see.** Monthly token and dollar allowances for the whole company
   and for each team. It shows a status when a team reaches **80%** and **100%** of its budget.
   **It never interrupts a developer's work.** It informs; it doesn't stop anyone.
3. **Prove: a record you can hand to finance.** Roles for owners, administrators, finance and
   viewers. A history of every administrative change. Spreadsheet exports for *chargeback*
   (billing each department for its own share).

> **Real estate comparison:** Ork Meter is the meter on one house. Ork Meter Enterprise is the
> property manager's report for the whole portfolio: every unit, every tenant, every bill,
> with a budget per building and a clean record for the accountant.

## Who it's for

Companies with many developers using AI agents. The buyer is usually **not** a developer.
It's someone responsible for money or for the whole engineering organization: a **CTO**,
**VP of Engineering**, **head of IT**, or someone in **finance** (CFO, controller, FinOps).

## How a customer buys it

- The first step is **a 30-minute walkthrough with a sample workspace**. The customer doesn't have
  to set anything up. Your job is to **book that demo**.
- Companies can start with a **pilot**: a short trial period on their own data before a full
  annual agreement.
- **Pricing is quoted by EDOS leadership, not published.** Never guess a price. Say: *"Pricing
  depends on your team size; Travis or Jeremy will put a proposal together after the walkthrough."*

## The 20-second pitch

> "Your company is paying for AI agents. Can finance tell you which team spent what last month?
> Ork Meter Enterprise gives you one view: usage and estimated cost by department and team,
> budgets every team can see, and a clean record for finance. It never slows a developer down.
> Can I set up a 30-minute walkthrough? No setup on your side."

## Important wording

Say it **informs, attributes and shows**: "visibility and ownership". **Don't** say it
"controls", "blocks" or "enforces" anything. It doesn't, and saying so would mislead the buyer.
See [Say this, not that](09-say-and-dont-say.html).
