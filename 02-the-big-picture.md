You don't need to understand how software works to sell ours. You need to understand
**the problem our customers have**. Here it is in plain words.

## What is an "AI coding agent"?

A **developer** (also called a software engineer, or an "engineer") writes the instructions,
the **code**, that make apps and websites work.

An **AI coding agent** is an assistant that a developer can talk to in plain English: *"Add a
search box to this page"* or *"Find out why checkout is broken."* The agent reads the project,
writes code, runs tests, and reports back. The two big names today are **Claude Code** (made by
Anthropic) and **Codex** (made by OpenAI). Lots of companies now give these to every developer.

> **Real estate comparison:** think of an AI agent as a very fast contractor. You tell it what
> you want done in the house, and it gets to work. It's good, but you still want to know what
> it's charging you, and you still want someone to inspect the work before you pay.

## What is a "token"?

AI agents are billed by the **token**. A token is a small piece of text, roughly a short word.
Everything the agent reads and writes is counted in tokens, and tokens cost money.

> **Comparison:** tokens are like the electricity meter on a house. Every light left on adds up.
> An agent that re-reads the same files over and over, or reads huge pages of output it doesn't
> need, is a house with every light on.

You'll hear two kinds: **cached** tokens (things the agent has already read recently, which are cheaper)
and **non-cached** tokens (new reading, the expensive kind). You don't need more detail than that.

## The problem companies have

Agents spread through companies quickly, often one developer at a time. Then the company discovers:

1. **"We can't see what it costs."** The bill shows up at the end of the month as one big number. Nobody
   knows which team, project or person drove it.
2. **"We can't manage it."** There are no budgets per team, and no warning before money runs out.
3. **"We can't prove what happened."** When something changes in a product, nobody can easily show
   which agent did it, who asked for it, or whether a person approved it.
4. **"We don't know how to do this well."** Teams want the speed of agents without the mess, and they
   don't have experts in-house.
