Your goal in almost every conversation is **one thing: book the next step** with the right
person. That might be a demo, a design-partner call, or a discovery call with Jeremy or Travis.

## Opening lines

- *"Are your developers using AI coding assistants like Claude Code or Codex yet?"*
- *"Quick question: if your CFO asked which team spent the most on AI last month, could anyone answer?"*
- *"A lot of companies we talk to love what AI agents can do but can't see what they cost. Is that true for you?"*

## Discovery questions (listen more than you talk)

1. How many developers do you have? How many use AI agents today?
2. Which tools: Claude Code, Codex, others?
3. Who pays the AI bill, and do they know where it goes?
4. Has anyone asked you to prove what an AI agent changed, or who approved it?
5. What would make this a priority this quarter?
6. Who else would be part of a decision like this?

> **Real estate habit that works here:** you'd never show houses before learning the budget,
> the must-haves and who signs. Same here: **pain, people, priority**.

## Handling common objections

| They say | You say |
|---|---|
| **"We're too small."** | "Ork Meter is free to start and built for individual developers. You can see value this week without talking to anyone." |
| **"Our AI vendor already shows usage."** | "Each vendor shows its own slice. Ork Meter puts Claude Code and Codex together, by project and team, with the dollar value next to it." |
| **"Is our code or data safe?"** | "Ork Meter collects usage information only; conversations stay on the developer's machine. Orkestera runs on your own infrastructure. I'll set up a call with our CTO for the details." |
| **"How much will it save us?"** | "We publish our test results, including the flat ones, so you can see the method. Every team is different, so we don't promise a number. A walkthrough will show what to expect for yours." |
| **"How much does it cost?"** | Ork Meter: quote the published prices from [edos.io](https://edos.io). Enterprise, Orkestera and consulting: "It depends on your team size and needs; we'll send a proposal after the walkthrough." |
| **"We'll build it ourselves."** | "You could. Our founders have twenty-plus years each and built this already. Most teams would rather spend their engineers on their own product." |
| **"Not right now."** | "Totally fair. Can I send you a short overview and check back next quarter?" Then actually follow up. |

## Closing for the next step

- *"Would a 30-minute walkthrough next Tuesday or Wednesday work better?"*
- *"Who else should be on that call: someone from finance, or your engineering lead?"*

Then send a short recap email the same day, with the date, who's coming and what they want to see.
Copy **info@edos.io** so the team sees it.

## When to hand off

Hand the conversation to **Jeremy (CTO)** or **Travis (CEO)** as soon as you hear any of:
security reviews, contracts, custom pricing, specific integrations, legal or compliance
requirements, or questions you can't answer from this course. **Saying "great question, let me get our CTO on
that" builds trust.** Guessing destroys it.
